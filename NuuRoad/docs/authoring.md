# Authoring guides

Open the tracker from the round minimap button (left-click; right-click opens the guide browser). Select **Menu -> Guides -> New guide** to start a personal guide, or, with a built-in guide active, choose **Menu -> Edit this guide** to make a personal copy named "Copy of ...". Packaged guides are never modified. A personal guide receives its ID when it is first saved, so cancelling the editor leaves nothing behind.

The editor shows the whole guide as a step list on the left and the selected step's form on the right. It provides these controls:

- guide title, and the **Folder** the guide browser files it in: folders split on `/`, any depth (`Leveling/The Barrens`, `Dungeons/Wailing Caverns`); left empty it is `Personal`;
- a step list with **Add step** (inserts after the selected step, in its section), **Delete**, **Up**, and **Down**;
- a schema node type chosen from a row of buttons (use `choice` for a branching step; a type the editor does not list, such as a cloned `acceptQuest` step, is shown on its own button and kept as it is);
- optional detail text, shown under the step in the tracker and in the step's tooltip;
- an optional map ID, X/Y as map percentages (52.26, 31.93), and arrival radius in the same units, which power the arrow and map pins; the editor converts to and from the normalized values the schema stores, and the arrow caps the arrival radius at 7 yards. The arrow window (an option under **Menu -> Options**) follows the tracker: closing the tracker closes it, and showing the tracker again brings it back. A waypoint of your own is travel rather than the guide, so its arrow stays up with the tracker closed, and a hearth or teleport on its route is still clickable in the arrow window. The arrow is a pre-rendered turn on a sprite sheet, so it picks the frame for the current heading instead of spinning a flat texture, and it colours itself red behind, amber across, green ahead; nothing about that is authored. It swaps itself out for an icon when pointing is not the answer: a hopping pin on arrival, a wing while the player rides a flight path, a barred ring when the step's map cannot be reached from where the player is. While the player is a ghost the arrow and the map pins leave the guide and point at the corpse instead ("Your corpse", arriving at reclaim range), the way Zygor and RestedXP do, and return to the step on resurrection. `/nr way [mapID] <x> <y>` points the arrow at a place the player types in, the way TomTom's /way does: X and Y as map percentages, on the map the player is on unless a map ID comes first (`/nr way 45.2 60.1`, `/nr way 1413 45.2 60.1`). The waypoint outranks the guide step (the corpse still comes first), shows the arrow window even with its option off, gets an unnumbered accent bead on the maps, and is put away on arrival within 10 yards or by `/nr way clear`; the arrow then goes back to the step. It is session-only and never touches the guide. Nothing about any of that is authored;
- branching choice labels and their destination step numbers;
- **Group + next** to join the selected quest visit, quest objective, or group with the next step/group, and **Ungroup** to make all members standalone; grouped rows show `[G]` and the form shows their step range;
- the step's NPC IDs, marked in the world when seen, and, for a kill step, the number of kills to count from the combat log;
- for the goal types (**Train**, **Skill**, **Gather**, **Craft**, **Skillup**, **Buy**, **Equip**, **Rep**, **Cast**), up to four fields: what the step is about, how much of it, the profession where one applies, and a rank that ends it early: a skill name and a rank, an item ID and a count, an item ID with a rank and a profession (**Skillup**: make it until that rank lands, however many that takes), or a faction ID and a standing written out ("Honored"). A **Buy** step takes its vendor from the NPC field and names them in the step. **Gather**, **Craft**, and **Buy** also have an **Or rank** field: the profession rank that makes the step moot, written as `100` for the profession beside it or `Blacksmithing 100` for another one. The step then finishes on the items or that rank, whichever comes first, which is what keeps a profession guide from asking for ore the player no longer needs. A rank escape on a step whose type has no such field is kept as it is. The step completes on its own when the game reports it done, and its title is generated from those fields until you edit the title yourself. Leaving a field blank leaves the step advancing by hand rather than with a broken rule; a TRAIN step with no rank means "learn it at all". **Train** also takes class spells by ID instead of a skill name ("100, 772"): the step completes once the client knows every one, and the trainer is the first NPC ID. **Cast** takes a spell ID and how many casts (one if blank), counted while the guide is on the step. These steps keep their NPC IDs too, so a trainer or vendor is still marked and still gets its star.

Steps whose title is generated from IDs (accept, turn in, kill, and so on) keep that generated title until you change it; editing the title marks it as custom while the IDs behind it are kept. Extra map pins (`spots`) on a cloned step are preserved but not editable yet.

For a choice, use **Add** under the choice pager, set its label, and enter the destination step number. The destination can be any existing step; a choice cannot be added to the last step until another step exists. Deleting a step removes any choice that led to it. Remove choices before changing a choice node back to a non-branching type.

Choose **Save and preview** to validate, save locally, and return to the tracker. A failed validation leaves the editor open and explains the first problem. Personal guides are account-wide; progress is session-only. Saving clears that guide's positional progress and scans it afresh. Its revision increases so an old saved resume position is discarded.

Packaged guides are written in guide format v1 (`docs/examples/guide-v1.lua`, with how each part behaves in `docs/guide-schema.md`): a list of sections, a `type` on every step, IDs only, NPC names in the guide's own `npcs` table, and labels rather than step numbers for branches. When adding a quest giver, add its creature ID, name and position to that table; the in-game tooltip lookup only answers for creatures the client has already seen this session, exactly as in RestedXP and Zygor, so the table is what keeps titles stable on a fresh login. Quests, items and spells are named by the client.

When porting quest IDs, turn on **Debug log** in **Menu -> Options**. Each auto-advance then reports the quest IDs the next step expects, with the client's title for each where it has one, and any quest accepted or turned in that is not among the current step's expected IDs.

The built-in guides were ported from another expansion, so an ID can point at something else on this client, or at nothing. While **Debug log** is on, the `[audit]` checks compare the client's names against the names the guides give (their `npcs`) and print, once per session, every quest, NPC, or item whose live name differs from the authored one. A quest of the current step that is offered or turned in at an NPC other than the one the step names is reported with that NPC's ID. Whatever you target, mouse over, talk to, hold in your bags, or carry in the quest log is also checked the other way round: a thing that carries a guide name under a different ID is reported with that ID, which is the value to put into the guide. Beyond IDs, the same checks report a quest handled, an objective advanced, or an item picked up far from the step's coordinates or on another map, an objective step whose quest line is about something else, has another count, or does not exist (a step that clicks a world object is judged only by whether another line of the quest names that object, since an object's name is rarely the objective's wording), and a hearthstone bound or a flight master met under a name other than the step's. `/nuuroad audit` checks every quest, item, cached NPC, and objective of the active guide at once, asks the server for anything uncached, and reports IDs that do not exist on this client. `/nuuroad debug` toggles the debug log from chat. Every finding is also kept in the saved variables; `/nuuroad log` opens a window with all of them as plain text, headed by the addon and client versions and grouped by guide (with its ID and revision) and character (with race, class, and level), one finding per line with the time and the step it was seen on, ready to select and paste elsewhere; `/nuuroad log clear` empties it.

Do not put macros, frame names, or scripts in guide fields, and never a Lua function: a guide table has to serialize into SavedVariables and validate, and a function does neither. New node types and their editor controls will be introduced together with their tracker presentation and fixture guide.

## Conditions

The editor's **Shown when** tab decides who sees a step. Leave it empty and everyone does.

Its field says what a guide's `cond` and `show` can say, as text: a class, race, or faction (`Warrior`, `Warrior or Shaman`, `Tauren`, `Horde`), `season` or `season 2`, `hardcore`, a profession the character knows (`skill Mining`, or its SkillLine ID, `skill 186`), and the two answers from Options (`ah`, `selffound`). Put `not` in front to turn a term around. Terms joined by `or` pass if any of them does; a bar between them means every part must pass, so `not Warrior | not Druid` is neither of the two. A term the parser does not know is refused and the step keeps the condition it had, with the reason in the status line. What a step works out from its own fields - an optional turn-in passed unless a quest is ready, an accept from an item passed when the item is not in the bags - is not on the form, and saving keeps it.

A condition the client cannot judge counts as met, so a step is never hidden by a check that could not run - which is what happens on this client whenever an API a predicate wanted is missing.

Two of the terms read answers only the player can give, under **Options -> About your realm**: whether they use the auction house, and whether they play self found. Until they answer, those conditions are unknown, so both branches of a guide that uses them stay visible.

## Carried steps

Some instructions should not stop the guide. On the editor's **Step** tab, **Carried alongside** names the step this one rides along with - `next` for the following step, or the label of one further on. The guide then never stops here: the step is drawn on the card under whichever step is current, and it is done when that step is. The toggle beside it keeps a step up alongside every step after it instead, until you tick it off, which is what a "watch out for this" line wants.

**Wait** takes the seconds a step keeps you standing there, for a scene or a timed quest; it is shown, never enforced.

**Item to use** takes the item IDs the step asks the player to press, which is often not the item the step counts: a Bael'dun step counts Broken Tools and presses Prospector's Picks. It gates and completes nothing - it puts the item in the item panel while the player is carrying it, and NuuRoad never presses it. `Fixture: Quest Items` has that step to test against.

When a step has several points, the arrow takes whichever is nearest. The **Destination** tab edits the first point; the others are set in the guide data.

## How a step looks in the tracker

Tooltips across the addon show at once. They say only what the control cannot: a caption that needs a tooltip to explain it wants rewording instead. Menu
entries carry none, since one would cover the entries beside it; a disabled drop-down entry says why it is disabled.
Quest and creature IDs appear in tooltips only with **Debug log** on.

The target panel and the item panel draw no background behind their contents: they are their icons over the world, with a plate behind the header bar alone, since that strip carries the title and the count and is what you grab to move the panel. Every line of text in the addon carries a hard shadow, which is what keeps a label readable with nothing behind it. The tracker, the materials list, and the windows that are read rather than played through - the guide browser, Options, the editor, and Diagnostics - keep their panel. The current step is a raised card. Inside it, every thing the player has to do is its own line, the way Zygor breaks a step down: "Talk to Tonga Runetotem", then "Turn in The Forgotten Pools", then "Accept The Zhevra". Each line carries an icon for its kind (quest available, quest turn-in, skull for kills, bag for items, arrow for travel, and so on; an ASCII glyph stands in where the client lacks the texture) and a tint for its live state: none until started, amber while in progress, green once the client reports it done - a finished line keeps the icon of what it asked for, the tint is what says it is behind the player, red when the player has no quest for it (not in the log and never finished). The guide does not pass such a step over: the card says "You do not have this quest", and the guide waits there until the quest is picked up or the player presses Skip. Quest names, NPC names, monsters, places, and counts are coloured by role; an item is the game's own link, so it wears its quality colour, shows the item tooltip on hover, and answers a click the way a link in chat does (Shift to drop it into chat). A kill or collect line shows the client's count, for example "(3/8)", and the number in front of the thing counts **down**: a step for eight beaks with four in the bag reads "Collect 4 Plainstrider Beak (4/8)", because the figure the player acts on is the one still owed. Once the count is met the whole figure comes back, so no line ever reads "Collect 0". A level line says what reaching the level still takes, the way Zygor counts a grind down: "Reach level 17 (4210 XP, ~23 kills)". The kill figure divides the experience still owed, less the rested bonus still to come (half the rested pool), by the average of the last five kills this session, their rested bonus taken off; before the first kill it assumes a mob of the player's own level (level x 5 + 45, times the realm rate from Options when given). A level past the next one is added up from the classic experience curve, scaled by what the client says the current level takes. A level step with `by` (the editor's **By step** field on the **Level** type) takes off the experience of the turn-ins still ahead before that step, so the line asks only for what the turn-ins will not bring; see `docs/guide-schema.md`, Level by a later step. Quest experience is learned account-wide from every quest offer and turn-in, so a second character on the same route sees every turn-in counted. A collect line whose step names the creatures the item drops from says so - "Collect 8 Windfury Talon from Windfury Harpy and Windfury Wind Witch" - since an item name on its own leaves the player with nowhere to go for it. An `object` says the same for an item that comes out of something on the ground ("Collect 4 Fungal Spores from Laden Mushroom"), and an objective step that is really a conversation reads "Talk to Great Bear Spirit" rather than the quest and an index. A `use` on a step that counts something leads that same line rather than taking one of its own, and takes the drop source with it, since the creatures carry what is pressed ("Use Prospector's Pick from Bael'dun Digger and Bael'dun Appraiser, and Collect 5 Broken Tools"). Hovering a line shows only what the line cannot say: the item's own tooltip, the quest and NPCs it names, and why a line is greyed or red; the distance is on the line and the note on the card, so neither is repeated, and a line that names nothing more has no tooltip. It never says which raid marker a creature will get: that is a guess until the game has actually put one on, and the target panel shows only the marker a creature is really wearing.

An explicitly authored quest group is shown as one card. In packaged guides, wrap its accept, turn-in, or combined visits in `{ group = { ... } }`; in the editor, use **Group + next**. The same wrapper puts quest work done in one place on one card: the kill, collect, and objective steps of a camp where the mobs mix, so every target is marked at once and each step keeps its own count. Ungrouped steps stay separate, even at the same NPC. The card is written per visit, a visit being a run of members at one NPC: they share a single "Talk to" line, and a visit that is a single quest drops that header and folds it into the quest - "Accept <quest> from <NPC>", "Turn in <quest> to <NPC>" - so a hub of six single-quest givers is six lines rather than twelve. A giver handing over more than one thing keeps its header with the quests under it. There is no distance rule or eight-step cap. Each member has its own completion and condition, and quests can be handled in any order as the game offers them. A member the game reports done leaves the card, so the group always reads as the work still in front of the player; the pointer advances through finished leading members, and the step the guide is actually on stays on the card however it reads. Card lines are drawn to the guide list's measure - same icon, text size, and row height - so the current step and the steps around it read as one list. The arrow and Back, Skip, and jump controls still refer to individual steps, and upcoming rows start after the remaining available group members. Each visit's first line also carries how far its step is, in a column on the right ("180 yd", "1.4k yd", "here"), re-read as the player moves: the members are errands the player picks an order for, so the distances are part of that choice. Quest visits standing in the same spot show it once, while each kill, collect, or objective step keeps its own reading even when it shares a camp with the others, and nothing outside the group - carried work, the sticky card, the upcoming rows - shows a distance at all. The guide list under the cards shows a group's members as plain rows: the card is the only place a group is drawn as one. Groups stay within one section and contain no branches or nested groups.

Inserting a standalone step inside a group splits it into the remaining consecutive runs (single members become standalone). Deleting a member shrinks its group. Moving within a group preserves membership; ungroup before moving across a group boundary. Branch destinations are adjusted when steps move, are inserted, or are deleted. Changing a grouped step to a kind a group cannot hold or adding a branch requires ungrouping first.

Carried work sits **above** the step, where Zygor and RestedXP put it, on a card of its own with a grey bar in place of the blue one: steps carried alongside this one (`with`), steps carried from an earlier label (`from`), and sticky steps (`sticky = true`), because to the player they are the same thing: work in hand while the step below says where to go next.

Under both cards is the guide itself, one step per row, each led by the step's number - the guide's own step number, the same for every character and the one the counter in the title bar, the jump box, and a bug report speak, so the numbers skip wherever the list leaves a step out (carried steps, and other characters' steps). A step whose `cond` fails on who the character is - class, race, faction, or the realm's season or hardcore rule - is not in the guide for that character at all: it has no row, and the bar and the guide browser's "done of" counts leave it out; the numbers are not rewritten around it. A step whose `show` does not hold when the guide gets there (a profession the character does not know, an answer under Options) applies only if it already holds then, so the flow passes over it, the way RestedXP skips a step on `.itemcount`; so does an optional turn-in with nothing ready to hand in, and an accept from an item that is not in the bags. It scrolls a whole step at a time - the wheel moves it one row, never part of one - and the hairline on the right says where in the guide the window sits. The row the player is on is picked out in the accent colour; rows the game reports as already done are green and dimmed, including steps finished out of order; a step the player has no quest for is red. Hovering a row says the step itself rather than what clicking it does: what it asks for goal by goal, with the same live marks the current card uses (a tick for done, a tilde for work under way, an x for a goal the player has no quest for), then the quests and creatures it names, its zone, coordinates and distance, any wait, the step's condition when it has one, and the step note. Under the step, a last line says when it belongs to a group or the guide waits on it. The carried card reads the same way, without the note it already shows under the line. Clicking a row ahead moves the guide there and records the steps in between as skipped, the way Skip does; clicking a row behind is navigation only and rewrites nothing. **Menu -> Options** sets how many steps are on screen (zero puts the list away). The same numbers mark the world map, the way RestedXP labels its pins: the step the player is on is a small dark disc with a thin accent ring, and the next steps with a place (five by default, set under **Menu -> Options -> Map pins ahead**, zero for none) are smaller translucent discs with no ring that fade a little further down the route. Pins stay the same size on screen however far the map is zoomed, and a click on one goes through to the map; only hovering it is theirs. Every point of a step carries its number. Steps already done get no pin (a step waiting on a quest the player does not have keeps its pin, since the guide stops there), and the pins stop at a choice, since the branch is the player's to pick. Pins close enough to overlap become one pin labelled with the earliest step and a plus ("12+"), and hovering any pin lists its steps by number and title, the current one in the accent colour. The minimap shows only the step the arrow points at, as its number alone in outlined accent text, with no disc behind it. Its points inside the minimap's range are drawn where they are; when none is in range, the nearest (the arrow's target) sits dimmed on the rim in its direction, the way RestedXP floats a far pin on the minimap's edge, and says so in its tooltip. The corpse keeps a plain red bead. The grip in the tracker's bottom-right corner does the same two things by hand: dragging across sets the tracker's width, and dragging down adds or takes away list rows a whole step at a time, down to none. Both are saved.

The bar under the title is the whole guide at a glance, as this character has it: one continuous strip with every step in order except the ones ruled out for the character (above), which take no room, so a section made only of those has no column either. Each step is painted for what it is to this character - green once the game reports it done (wherever it sits, ahead of the player or behind), amber where the player does not meet the step's condition yet (only with **Menu -> Options -> Mark steps not open yet on the bar**, off by default; off, such a step is painted blue or grey like the steps around it), blue for a step already passed but not done (skipped, or passed by a jump), and grey for what is still ahead. A step that is both done and gated out is painted as done; a step the player has no quest for is not singled out, since picking the quest up fixes it. A run of one state is one piece, so the grey stretches are the work that is really left. A small bright mark stands on the step the player is on; it moves with Back, Skip, and a jump as well as with real progress. Behind the strip, each section of a guide with more than one is a column the full height of the bar in a jewel tone of its own - sapphire, amethyst, garnet, teal-blue, indigo, then round again. The section the player is in wears its tone stronger, edged top and bottom, and the title bar names it after the guide ("Horde Barrens  > Crossroads"). The columns are taller than the strip and none of them is green or amber, so a section never reads as a run of steps. The green lines and the bar are read from the game on every repaint, and a quest, objective, or level that lands out of order repaints the tracker even though the guide did not advance, so work done ahead of the current step shows up as it happens. Repaints from game events are coalesced, and a closed tracker does none. A quest accepted or handed in at a step further down the guide than the one the player is on - and not one carried alongside it, which the step card already shows - is also called out in chat: "Out of order: the guide picks up Cry of the Thunderhawk at step 5, and you are on step 1." The client says for itself that a quest was accepted or turned in, so the line adds only what it cannot: that the guide wanted this one later. Nothing else about quests is announced.

The bar is also a scrubber: clicking it moves the guide to the step under the cursor, and holding the button down drags through the guide step by step, with a tooltip naming the step the cursor is over, the section it belongs to when the guide has more than one, and its state (done, not for this character, no quest, or passed without being done). Its hit area is taller than the bar. A scrub is the same navigation as the jump box - nothing is recorded as skipped, no status is rewritten, and the step it settles on is held the same way as after Back or Skip.

Every control the tracker has sits in its title bar: the guide's name, then the step counter with **Back** (`<`) and **Skip** (`>`) on either side of it, then the menu and close. Back, Skip, and the menu carry no plate of their own - a mark on the title bar, muted until the cursor is on it - so the numbers they flank stay the thing the eye lands on. The exception is Skip on a step only the player can move on - one with no completion rule (a note, a vendor visit, a click), or one the game already reports done or settled - where it lights up in the accent colour on a slowly pulsing backdrop. A step with no completion rule also ends its card with a **Done - next step** button, which records the step as done and moves on, where Skip records it as skipped (not on the closing step, or a step with choices to pick); the menu wears the client's cog, or `=` where that art is missing. There is no minimize button for now. The menu is where everything that is not a step away lives - **Guides**, **Edit this guide**, **Reset this guide** (which asks for the click twice), **Materials list** on a guide that names any, **Authoring**, **Report a bug**, and **Options**. **Authoring** opens a submenu beside it, on hover or click, with the tools for writing guides rather than playing them: **Session log**, **Place recorder**, and **Diagnostics**. No entry ends in `...`: every one opens its window at once. A click anywhere else closes the menu. There is no second row of buttons and nothing opens under the tracker: the step note is already on the card and in the step's tooltip, and Diagnostics is a window of its own.

The step counter in the tracker's title bar ("9 / 45") is the guide's own step number out of all its steps, and a jump box: clicking it takes a step number and moves the guide straight to that step, forward or back (a number that belongs to another character's step lands on the next one of yours). A jump is navigation only - it records no step as skipped and rewrites no status - and the step it lands on is held the same way as after Back or Skip, so one already finished is not advanced past on arrival. Press Enter to jump, Escape to close the box. A number outside the guide is refused with a chat line.

### Picking a guide up in the middle

Loading a guide scans its steps against what the client still knows, and the guide opens on the first step that is still outstanding. Only durable evidence counts: quest history, the quest log, and the log's objective lines (so an objective is credited both when its line reads as finished on a quest still in the log and when the quest has already been turned in), the player's level, the hearthstone's binding, and known flight paths. Kill counters, bag contents, and the player's position say nothing about what happened earlier, so steps that rest on those are never claimed by the scan; where one sits before a step the scan did prove, it is recorded as skipped, the same as pressing Skip, so nothing unobserved is ever shown as complete. The guide then opens on the first step that is genuinely still to do: a step whose rule the client can judge either way (a quest, an objective, a level, or a branch to pick) and that is not done stops the resume even when finished steps lie past it, while a step the client keeps no record of - travel, kills, loose items, a hearth, a flight path, a note - never holds the resume point and is passed over as skipped. A collect step tied to a quest (`starts` for an item that begins a quest, `quest` for a quest item) is judged through that quest instead: it holds the resume point until the quest is accepted (or, for a quest item, done), and a quest item for a quest the player does not have holds it too. So does any step for a quest the player does not have, and any step whose condition could still open: the scan never resumes past work the player might yet take up. Finished steps past the resume point are left on the tracker to paint, as a green line and a green band. A step the client cannot judge (travel, a note, a kill count) is painted as settled - the same green line, and "Not needed" on the current step - once the first step after it that the client can judge is proven, since its work no longer matters; an outstanding quest in between keeps it open. The scan repeats over the first few quest-log updates after login, because the client hands the quest log over a few frames late. While you play, a travel step or a note is passed over, recorded as skipped, once the quest step it leads to is done: an accept, a turn-in, or an objective, looking through any run of travel steps and notes in between. A step that hands one quest in and takes the next counts, and so does an optional turn-in now passed over because the quest was handed in early, out of order: the travel to it has nothing left to lead to. Walking to the NPC by another path and taking the quest is arrival enough. A hearth binding, a known flight path, a level, or items in the bags never pass one, since they can be older than the trip, and neither does a quest you never took. A note that matters beyond the step right after it belongs in that step's `note`, since the note step itself can be passed unseen. **Reset** still starts a guide from the top. **Back** and **Skip** move exactly one step, the way Zygor's arrows do, and hold the step they land on while it reads done, so a finished step is not passed over again the moment you arrive. The hold is not a mode. It is judged the moment you land: a step still to do is not held at all, and a held step lets go as soon as it reads incomplete (a quest abandoned, a travel step you are not standing on). Either way, doing the step then advances the guide as usual; quests of the step's group are credited meanwhile. Shift-Back and Shift-Skip move one step and then keep going past every step the game reports done or settled, to the first step still to do (a step for a quest the player does not have, or one not open yet, counts as still to do and stops them) in that direction, or to the start of the trail or the end of the guide. They hold nothing, so Shift-Back to the start of a guide whose first steps are all done flows forward again on its own. A step Shift-Skip passes because the game reports it done is recorded as observed; one passed as settled is recorded as skipped. The jump box, the scrubber, and a click on an upcoming row hold the step they land on the same way. Guide progress is kept for the session only: nothing per guide is saved, and the scan places the player afresh at every login.

For authors this means: keep `npc` on accept and turn-in steps so the "Talk to" line appears, keep `kill`, `collect`, and `count` on objective steps so the tracker can name the target and show a count, put `starts` on a collect step whose item begins a quest and `quest` on one that gathers a quest item so the step is judged by the quest log rather than by bags, and put anything the icon and coloured line cannot say in `note`.

## Materials and the shopping list

A step can name the materials it needs on the editor's **Materials** tab, one
per line: an item ID, how many, and where it comes from (`gather`, `vendor`,
`ah`, `drop`, or `craft`; `gather` if left out), for example `2840 x10 gather`.
Counts are the whole step's, not per item made.

The tracker shows the current step's own materials under its goals, with what
is still missing from the bags. The **Materials list** entry in the menu - offered only on a
guide that names materials at all, with the number still missing beside it -
opens the list beside the tracker: everything the steps from here to the end
ask for, added up per item, grouped by where it comes from, ticked once the
player carries enough. Finished steps and steps this character is gated out of
ask for nothing. A step that is only partly done still counts in full, since
the guide cannot know how much of its work is left.

A guide whose every section buys its own materials first can keep the list to
**the section the player is in** (`meta.materials = "section"`): the list then
stops at the section's end and its title names the section. The editor's
Materials tab has the toggle for it ("The list shows only the current
section"); it is guide-wide, not per step. A profession route split by tier,
recipe or rank band sets it by itself.

None of it touches the auction house: `ah` is a label saying where to get the
item, and the player does the shopping. NuuRoad keeps no prices; it never
reads the auction house and posts no auctions.

## The profession planner

The profession planner is its own addon now, NuuProfessions (`../NuuProfessions/PROF_PLAN.md`). When it is
installed, the **Make** button on a `craft` or `skillup` step asks it how many crafts the step needs; without it the button makes as many as the materials allow and stops at the
skill the step names.

## Farming circuits

A gold or gathering circuit is a loop rather than a line: the route runs a
lap, then runs it again until the player has enough. Author it as a `choice`
whose first option jumps back to the first step of the loop and whose second
carries on past it (`docs/guide-schema.md` has the shape). Taking the backward
option starts a new lap: the steps it covers go back to undone and their
counts (kills, crafts, NPCs spoken to) start over, so the tracker asks for the
same work again instead of showing a lap that is already ticked off. The steps
before the loop and after the exit are untouched, Back still walks back
through the laps already run, and the step count in the title bar reads
`Lap 2`, `Lap 3` and so on from the second lap. Always give the loop an exit:
there is no repeat count and no automatic end to a circuit.

## Finding a guide

**Menu -> Guides** (or a right-click on the minimap button) opens the guide browser: three panes, places to look on the left, the guides in the middle, the chosen guide on the right.

The left pane starts with four views that need no idea where a guide is filed. **Suggested** lists guides that cover your level and that you have not finished. **Near me** lists guides with a step on the map you are on. **In progress** is the running guide and any other you have made progress in this session. **My guides** is every guide you wrote or copied. Under them are the **Folders**, the tree the guides' own `category` paths make, as deep as authors write them. A click on a folder lists every guide beneath it, subfolders included, and opens it; a second click folds it again. Each guide row names the folders under the one shown, so a guide filed deeper is never a mystery. The numbers beside every entry count what it holds.

The search box looks through every guide at once, whatever is picked on the left. Each word must match: title, folders, tags, author, the zones the guide's steps are in, and the titles of its quests (`quest:<id>` works too). A number is a level, matching a guide whose range holds it, so `barrens 15` finds the Barrens guide for a level 15 character. Clicking the tree clears the search.

**Only guides for this character** (ticked each session) leaves out guides whose own `cond` rules this character out: another faction, race, or class. A view that hid some says how many. Untick it and those guides come back, marked **not for this character**. A guide's level range is plain inside your level, amber above it, and muted below it.

A click on a guide only chooses it: the right pane shows its folders, levels, who it is for, how many sections, steps, and quests it has, the progress this session, where it came from, and its sections. **Start** (**Resume** once you have progress, **Show** for the running one) runs it in the tracker, as does a double-click on the row. **Edit** opens it in the editor; a packaged guide opens as a copy. **New guide** starts one of your own. Fixture guides stay out of the tree unless the debug log is on, but a search finds them.

## Finding the guides a quest is in

Select a quest in the quest log and a small button appears at the top right of its text, the way RestedXP's and Zygor's quest-log buttons do: **In guide**, **In 2 guides**, or, dimmed, **No guide**. Its tooltip names each guide that handles the quest (the active one marked "(active)") and what each of its steps does with it - "accepted at step 3, worked on at step 5, turned in at step 8"; a quest item or an item that begins the quest counts as "item picked up". Clicking the button opens the guide browser searched for the quest, so those guides are the whole list and any of them can be started from there. The same button sits on a quest offer while an NPC is proposing the quest, so whether a guide covers it is known before accepting. Every guide counts: built-in, personal, and (in the list) fixtures. Nothing is changed by any of this - no guide is selected, no step is jumped to.

The same lines are appended to every quest tooltip the client shows, so nothing has to be opened: hovering a quest link in chat or a quest row in the quest log adds "NuuRoad: Horde Barrens" when one guide handles the quest, "NuuRoad: in 2 guides" with one guide per line under it (the active one marked "(active)") when several do, or "NuuRoad: not in any of your guides"; only the addon's name is coloured, and the steps are only on the button's tooltip. A quest log row that has no tooltip of its own (the client shows one there only for party progress) gets one with the quest's title and the guide lines.

The guide browser's search box also matches quest titles and `quest:<id>` tokens, so typing a quest's name lists the guides that handle it without opening the quest log.

Authors need do nothing: the index is built from the quest IDs on the steps (`accept`, `turnin`, `talk`, a step's `quest`, `collect` with `starts` or `quest`) and, for a hand-written node, from its completion rule.

## NPC conveniences

The IDs on a step also drive a handful of optional conveniences, each a toggle under **Menu -> Options -> NPC conveniences**; the first three are paused while Shift is held, and a dialog opened or touched with Shift down stays the player's until it closes, so letting go of Shift to click a quest line does not hand the offer back to the addon:

- **Mark the step's NPCs.** Kill targets get a skull (cross, square, and so on for the second, third, ... kind listed) and quest NPCs a star. The NPCs of every quest step in the current run (see above) count, so a whole hub's quest givers get their star at once. A turn-in NPC whose quest is not in the log yet gets no star until it is, and neither does an NPC whose steps are all done. The client no longer lets an addon set a marker on its own, so every marker comes from an action of the player's: clicking a slot of the target strip targets that creature and marks it, the strip's **Mark all** button walks the whole list, and stays pressable while anything is still unmarked, so it can be pressed again for creatures that were out of reach, and two key bindings (Key Bindings > AddOns > NuuRoad, or `/nuuroad markkey` and `/nuuroad markallkey`) do the same from the keyboard. All of this is macro text behind secure buttons; NuuRoad creates no character macro for it and takes no macro slot. A creature that already carries its marker is left out of all of them, since the marker command toggles. Where the client still allows it, the marker is also set automatically on sight. In a raid only the leader and assistants can mark, so nothing happens there for anyone else.
- **Accept the current step's quests.** At a gossip or greeting window NuuRoad picks a quest the current authored quest group accepts, and accepts it when the offer opens. Every other quest the NPC offers is left to the player. A follow-up the NPC does not offer yet cannot be picked, so a chain keeps its order by the game's own rules. A quest is accepted or handed in only when the game names the open quest by ID and that ID is one of the step's: quests share titles, so a title alone never counts. On an old-style gossip or greeting list, which gives no IDs, NuuRoad may pick a line by its title, but that only opens the quest; if what opens is not the guide's quest, or the game gives no ID for it, the window is left to you.
- **Turn in the current step's quests.** A finished quest the current authored quest group turns in is picked at the NPC and handed in; an unfinished one is left alone. A quest with more than one reward to choose from stays open; a chat line says so.
- **Select the step's quest in the log.** When the guide reaches a step whose quest is already in the quest log, that quest becomes the log's selection, so opening the log lands on its page and the quest log button beside it answers for the same quest, the way Zygor and RestedXP point the log at the step in front of you. A step that names several quests picks the one you still have work on: an objective before a turn-in, and an accept last, since a quest that is not in the log yet is never selected - it is selected the moment it arrives. The selection moves once per step, so a quest you click yourself stays selected until the guide moves on. Nothing is accepted, abandoned, shared, or watched.
- **Sell junk at vendors.** On by default. Opening any vendor sells every grey item in the bags that the vendor pays for, and a chat line says how many went and what they came to. Where the client has the game's own Sell All Junk (`C_MerchantFrame.SellAllJunkItems`, the vendor window's button) NuuRoad uses it; otherwise it sells one stack every fifth of a second, checking that each slot still holds what it read, and stops when the vendor closes. Holding Shift as the vendor opens leaves the visit to you, and pressing Shift part way through stops the rest. Nothing better than grey is sold, and everything sold is on the vendor's Buyback tab.

- **Show the step's target strip.** A small movable panel beside the tracker with one portrait per creature of the current authored quest group, five to a row and wrapping onto as many rows as the step needs, the way RestedXP's active-target frame does it: the picture is the whole control, and the name (red for kill targets, green for NPCs to talk to), the kind, the progress, and the marker are in the tooltip. Rows stay in guide order, kill targets first, and never reorder: a slot keeps its creature, so its key binding, its portrait and its place in the marking order stay put while the player changes target. A creature the client cannot see is held back by a dimmer picture rather than by being moved (only the picture fades; the slot's plate and edge stay as they are), and **Only creatures in view** drops those rows entirely. A portrait is filled in the first time the unit is targeted, moused over, on a nameplate, or in a dialog, and until then a placeholder stands in. Once drawn, a creature's picture stays for the session: when the strip changes - a creature finishes and the rest move up, or the step moves on - a creature that moves to another slot takes its head with it rather than going back to the placeholder (on a client with no 3D models the flat portrait needs the unit again, so there it waits to be seen). The raid marker rides in the top-left corner of the picture, and only once the creature really carries one - the panel never shows the marker it is going to try for, because the client can give a different one or none at all. Each creature of the step gets its own icon - skull, cross and so on for kill targets, then the star and the rest for quest givers - so a hub of five givers is five different markers rather than five stars. A creature keeps its marker for as long as it stays in the group: when a member finishes or the step moves on, the creatures still listed keep the icons they had, and only a creature new to the group takes one, the first one free. Where the client will not report what a creature wears - this one reports only that a marker is absent, never which one is present - the icon the client draws is copied onto the slot instead, texture and coordinates together and neither of them read, so a portrait wears the creature's real marker - one the player set by hand included - while NuuRoad still does not know which it is. The step line draws it immediately in front of the name it belongs to, so a line naming several creatures shows each one's marker against its own name. The strip and the step lines answer different questions, so they can disagree, deliberately. A portrait shows any marker the creature is really carrying, whoever put it there, and clears the moment the client says the creature is bare. A step line shows only a marker a press through NuuRoad put on - a slot click, the Mark all press, or either mark key, all four of which record what they did - because the line reports what the guide asked for and what came of it; a creature marked by hand gets an icon on the strip and none on the line. Which marker is drawn is the one the step asked for either way: this client reports that a marker is present but never which one, so a marker changed by hand to a different icon still shows as the step's own (the routes tried are in `docs/api-compatibility.md`). The record lasts the session, dropped only when the creature dies, the step moves past it, or the client says plainly that it carries nothing. A creature that plainly carries something is never offered a marker again, since the press would toggle off the one it has. A creature the client will not name but plainly carries something is never offered a marker again, since the press would toggle off the one it has. A marker read off a unit is remembered for the session against the creature, not against the unit in view, so the icon stays put when the nameplate goes away and only another look at that creature changes it; a second press never toggles the mark back off. A creature whose work is done leaves the panel and loses the marker NuuRoad set: a kill target once every step naming it is done (its count reached, or its quest objective finished, in any order within the group), a quest NPC once every step naming it is done by its quests. A quest NPC with nothing to do yet - a turn-in for a quest the player does not have and never finished - is never on the panel: such a turn-in is not a member of the current group until its quest is in the log, often from an accept earlier in the same group, so it is neither on the group card nor offered a marker. Every marking press - a slot, Mark all, either key - clears the creature's marker and then sets the step's, so it sets rather than toggles. `/tm` on its own toggles, and nothing here can know what a creature is already wearing (this client will not say), so a press built on that guess could take a marker off instead of putting one on and then believe it was on. Pressing twice is the same as pressing once. The panel's title is itself the **mark all** control - click "Targets" to target and mark every creature of the step still needing its marker, one after the other; it is underlined while there is something to mark, and its tooltip says how many are waiting and which key does the same. A press counts every creature it tried as marked. It cannot be told which of its blocks found their creature, and "in view" means a nameplate, which the player may have turned off while standing in front of all of them; a claim that turns out to be wrong costs nothing, because the first look at that creature reads it as bare and offers it again. Clicking a slot targets that creature by name and marks it, through a secure macro button, and the first ten slots can be bound to keys under Key Bindings > AddOns > NuuRoad. Each slot is a picture on its own dark plate inside a ring of shadow, so a portrait keeps its edges over snow or sky, and it carries what the tooltip would otherwise have to be opened for: its key in the top-right corner, and at its foot how many of that creature are still wanted, or "talk" for one to speak to. The panel cannot change during combat, so a step that completes mid-fight repaints it once combat ends.
- **Show the step's item strip.** The same panel for items, also five to a row: the things the current step, its group, and its carried steps name, kept to the ones actually in the player's bags that the step names with `use`, or that the game says have a use, or that the step marks with `starts`, since a quest-starting item carries no use spell for the client to report and is exactly the item such a step is about (RestedXP writes the same thing as `.use`). The icon is the whole control, the item's own tooltip explains it, the bag count sits in the corner, and the border takes the item's quality colour. The slot carries its key in the top-left corner, the bag count over a strip of shade at its foot, and a gold corner for an item that begins a quest. A click uses the item and the first ten slots can be bound to keys, which is the point of the panel - reaching a quest item without digging through the bags. NuuRoad never uses one for the player.

Kills of a step's targets are counted from the combat log (killing blows by the player, their pet, or their party) where the client allows it, and otherwise from the player's own target dying while they fight it, which is what completes a `kill` step and what the "(2/3)" on it shows; a quest objective step keeps using the quest log's count. A creature the tracker has marked wears its raid icon on the step line too, right in front of its name: the icon the mob is carrying now, read back from the unit, never the one NuuRoad means to set, and gone again when the mark comes off or the creature leaves view - the same rule the target strip follows. A "Talk to" line on a quest visit is judged by the quests it is there for, never by the dialog, the same way the target strip judges a quest NPC: opening a gossip window and walking away turns it amber (work under way), and it goes green only once every quest under it is accepted or handed in. A plain `talk` step with no quest behind it is still finished by the dialog itself. `Fixture: NPC Tracking` (visible in the guide browser with the debug log on) exercises all of it around the Crossroads, including a group of two kill steps with a count each, an authored group of three accepts shown as one card and a turn-in NPC that waits for its quest.

## Travel routes

When a step is far away, the arrow and the tracker suggest the fastest sensible way there as a list of waypoints, one at a time in the arrow: walk to a flight master and fly, take a boat or a zeppelin, or leave a city by the right gate. The arrow window names the leg ("Fly to Ratchet") with the step under it; every waypoint still ahead gets a bead on the world map, and the one the arrow follows is on the minimap. Under the current step one muted line reads the whole route and what walking would take, for example `Route: fly Camp Taurajo > walk (~1m23s, walking ~3m20s)`. When there is another route to pick, the line ends with it (`- click: hearth > walk ~40s`): **click the line to take that route** for this step, and again for the next one, round to the suggestion. In the arrow window, **right-click the arrow** to pick from every route on offer, with its time, or to clear a `/nr way` waypoint. That is where to pick when the arrow follows a waypoint with no guide open. Any other click on the arrow window goes through to the world under it; **shift-drag** it to move it.

Only flight paths the character has learned are used, and at the flight master the destination is highlighted; the flight is never picked for you. The hearthstone is never the suggested route, since guides plan when to use it: when it is ready and clearly faster it is offered on the route line instead. A teleport that costs nothing to use, such as Teleport: Moonglade or a mage's teleports, can be the suggested route itself. One on cooldown is not offered at all. On a hearth or teleport leg the arrow window shows the hearthstone or the spell in the arrow's place, and clicking it there uses it; a change during combat waits until the fight ends. A route is only suggested when it saves a minute and a quarter of the walk, and once shown it stays until another is clearly better, so it does not flicker. Flight times are learned as you fly.

**Menu -> Options -> Travel routes** turns suggestions off altogether, or leaves out flight paths, the hearthstone and teleports, or boats and zeppelins, and turns off the flight master highlight. Caves a guide describes (below) are followed either way.

The world model is `Data/Travel.lua` (flight masters, transports, teleports, inns, and the walled areas with their gates). Rows there come from recordings: with recording on, every flight master opened writes a `flightpath` row with where you stood, and every change of map on foot writes a `door` row; `python3 Tools/SessionLog/doors.py` turns those into candidate rows to review.

## Places (caves and tunnels)

A place describes the way through a cave, a tunnel, or anything the arrow should not cut straight through (see "Places" in `docs/guide-schema.md` for the format). The easy way to make one is to walk it.

**Menu -> Authoring -> Place recorder** opens a small window of its own, so the editor is out of the way. Stand outside the mouth, press **Record**, walk every passage, and come back out. There is nothing else to press: it takes a sample every three yards, and whenever the indoors, falling or swimming flag or the map changes, and makes no point while you walk. The place is worked out from the walk afterwards:

- A point every 15 yards and at every turn. Walking back over a passage adds nothing, and coming round a loop or into a side passage joins up by itself. Nothing is kept outdoors.
- The ways in and out come from the game's indoors flag. A drop you fall down becomes a one-way connection, and falling out of the cave makes where you land an exit.
- The game gives no height, so two passages that run over each other are joined where they cross. For now, check such a spot in the Places tab and **Disconnect** it.
- **Mark** is optional: it names the spot you stand on ("the bank" becomes a point with the ID `bank`, which a step can stand on as `place.bank`).
- **Indoors only** off keeps the outdoor samples too, for a tunnel or ravine the game counts as outdoors; the place is worked out again from the same walk.

Every sample goes to the session log the moment it is taken, so a reload or a crash mid-walk loses nothing. **Stop** works the place out once so the map and the count show it, and names it after the subzone unless you typed a name. **To editor** adds it to the guide open in the editor (or the one you are following) and opens its Places tab; **Lua** shows it as guide source to paste into a guide file. The recorder keeps the raw walk, not the place: `python3 Tools/SessionLog/places.py` writes each walk in the session log to `Tools/SessionLog/places/`, and `lua Tools/ProcessPlace.lua Tools/SessionLog/places/<file>.lua snap=4 spacing=10` works the place out again with other settings.

The editor's **Places** tab fixes what the recorder made and builds places by hand. **Record** there opens the recorder on that place, continuing from the selected point. **Add point here** drops a point where you stand, joined to the selected one (the first point of a place is its entrance). Click a point to select it and shift-click a second: **Connect** joins them both ways, **One-way** only from the first to the second, and **Disconnect** parts them. Each point has a label (what the arrow says), an ID (what steps call it), and a kind button that cycles inside / entrance / exit. **Delete point** removes a point and renumbers the rest. The place is drawn on the world map while the tab or the recorder is open: entrances in the accent colour, exits in red, one-way connections with an arrowhead. Anything the validator objects to is listed under the buttons.

In the **Destination** tab a step can **stand on a place point** (`cave.chamber`, which fills the coordinates) or say which point its own coordinates are **near**, so the arrow knows which passage it is in when two levels lie over each other. The tab lists the guide's named points.

## Resuming where you left off

Settings, window placements, personal guides and drafts are kept in the saved variables. A setting is saved only
once you change it (a window placement once you drag the window); one never changed is not saved and follows the
addon's default, even when a later version changes that default. Choosing a value saves it even when it equals the
default. In **Menu -> Options** every setting you have changed shows **reset** after its control (its tooltip
names the default): a click forgets your choice. **Windows** has **Reset window positions**, which forgets where every
window stands (and the tracker's width) and puts the minimap button back; a window already open goes back to its
place the next time it is made. **Reset all**
beside Done (click twice to confirm) does every reset on the page. Of guide progress only the active guide's current step is kept, per character, written at logout together with the guide's revision; the next login opens the guide on that step unless the guide was edited since. Everything else about progress is session-only, and the login scan still runs from the resumed step, so quests finished in the meantime move the guide on as usual. `/nuuroad db` shows the saved step and whether it was used. Older versions kept this in a character macro named `NuuRoadResume` while the beta client did not restore saved variables; it is no longer read or written and can be deleted.

## Recording a session

**Menu -> Options -> Recording -> Record what I do** writes down what you do while you play, so a session can be turned into a guide afterwards. It is on unless you turn it off, and the same toggle sits at the top of the log window, so it can be switched without opening the options.

What is written: the whole quest log as it stands when recording starts, each quest with its description, objective text, and objective counts, so everything after it has a baseline; quests accepted, turned in, and abandoned; objective progress as the quest log reports it; loot of quest items (ordinary loot is not recorded); items crafted, used, and equipped; kills that counted for something; NPCs talked to; flight paths learned, flights taken and where they ended; the hearthstone being set and used; trainers, vendors, and repairs; levels gained; dying and releasing. Zone and subzone changes are not written; they happen at every road crossed and say little. Every row carries the map you were on and where you stood on it, which is what makes the log usable as route material rather than as a diary.

**Menu -> Authoring -> Session log** opens the log itself: one row per record, read the way the guide is read rather than as a wall of text - an icon for the kind, a one-line summary, the time it happened, and the rest (the quest, the NPC, the item, the map and the coordinates) in the tooltip when you hover the row. The newest row is at the bottom and the window follows the end of the log; scroll up with the wheel and it stays where you put it until you scroll back down. The count in the corner says how many rows the log holds.

The setting survives a logout like every other option, so recording is still on the next time you log in. The log is kept in the saved variables across sessions, and records older than **days kept in the log** (under the toggle, 10 unless changed) are dropped at every login; **Clean up** beside it drops them now. `Tools/SessionLog/sessions.py` reads the log straight out of the saved file outside the game; see `Tools/SessionLog/README.md`. Beside the log is the trace, a developer's debug log kept by login or reload rather than by the days setting: every session that traced something in the last 24 hours, and at least the last three, up to 10000 rows in all; `sessions.py --trace` reads the latest, `--list-traces` lists what is kept. Nothing is sent anywhere, and nothing is recorded while the toggle is off.

## Reporting a bug

**Menu -> Report a bug** opens a small form. Pick **The guide** for a problem with the content (a wrong place, a wrong quest, NPC or item, a step that will not finish or finished early, the order, the text) or **The addon** for a problem with NuuRoad itself (tracker, arrow and map pins, travel route, quest accept and turn-in, target marks and the NPC panel, items and materials, the editor, saved data, a Lua error). Pick the category, check the **Step** number (it starts on the step in front of you; change it if the problem was on another step), and say in a line what you expected and what happened. For a Lua error, paste the error text.

**Report** gathers the rest: the guide and step with each goal's live status, the rule that finishes it, its coordinates and how far away you are, the steps around it and the ones you passed to get there, what the client says about the step's quests, where you stand, what you have targeted, the last rows of the session log, and for addon reports your settings and the diagnostics of the part you picked (navigation, travel, marking, saved data, client API). Each part is read on its own, so a broken part of the addon shows as one line and never stops the report.

The report is written to the `bug` channel of the session log; nothing opens in the game, and the form says how many reports this session has saved. It reaches disk at logout or /reload. Outside the game, `python3 Tools/SessionLog/sessions.py --bugs` prints every report still in the log, line for line. A report is dropped with the rest of the log once it is older than the days kept.
